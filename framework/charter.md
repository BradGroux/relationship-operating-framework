# Relationship Operating Framework charter

- **Status:** Accepted 1.0.0
- **Founding steward:** Brad Groux
- **Effective date:** 2026-08-03

## Definition

The Relationship Operating Framework is an open documentation framework for
stewarding real relationships across time. It helps people and their teams
maintain truthful continuity, honor commitments, respect consent and boundaries,
re-engage without manufacturing obligation, and use assistance without giving
away human responsibility.

A relationship is a human reality shaped by shared experience, trust,
commitments, consent, boundaries, and change. A repository, memory, CRM entry,
message history, or score may contain information about a relationship; it is
not the relationship itself.

## Mission

Help people care for long-running relationships with the same attention they
give new opportunities: contribute before asking, remember what matters, honor
what was promised, make room for change, repair harm when possible, and let
people decline or disengage without penalty.

## Who it serves

The framework serves individuals and teams who carry relationships through
professional, community, partner, customer, collaborator, mentor, peer,
education, open-source, and other contexts.

It can help a person acting alone, a team sharing stewardship, or an assisting
agent working under a named person's authority. It does not require AI,
automation, a CRM, or any particular organizational structure.

## Scope

The framework covers:

- truthful, proportionate context;
- continuity across time, role changes, and handoffs;
- explicit and implied commitments that require human confirmation;
- consent, communication preferences, privacy, and boundaries;
- contribution before asks without creating a debt;
- non-transactional re-engagement;
- follow-through, repair, waiting, closure, and do-not-contact decisions;
- accountable human judgment and bounded agent assistance; and
- learning that improves stewardship without ranking people.

The framework provides non-sequential questions and practical guidance. People
apply them within their existing lives, responsibilities, communities, and
organizational processes.

## Commitments

1. People are never reduced to opportunities, assets, records, or scores.
2. Relationship continuity matters more than contact frequency or visible
   activity.
3. Existing commitments come before new asks.
4. Contribution creates no entitlement to attention, access, reciprocity, or
   future business.
5. Re-engagement is an invitation that can be ignored or declined without cost.
6. Consent and communication boundaries are current, specific, and controlled
   by the affected person.
7. Context is retained only when there is a legitimate stewardship need and an
   appropriate place to keep it.
8. Repair, waiting, closure, and do not contact are legitimate outcomes.
9. A named person owns judgment and every external action, even when a team or
   agent assists.
10. Methods should outlast the tools used to support them.

## Anti-scope

The framework does not:

- tell people whom to befriend, influence, target, contact, or sell to;
- define a sales methodology, funnel, pipeline, lead score, relationship score,
  or conversion model;
- specify a CRM, database, schema, protocol, API, agent contract, runtime,
  automation system, communication platform, or conformance layer;
- authorize scraping, enrichment, inferred intimacy, surveillance, covert
  personalization, or mass outreach;
- allow an agent or tool to decide or perform external contact autonomously;
- make contribution a tactic for acquiring access or obligation;
- require a universal relationship lifecycle, maturity model, status taxonomy,
  first-user profile, first-job definition, or domain model;
- prescribe Mission Control software, views, metrics, or data structures;
- replace legal, privacy, safety, safeguarding, organizational, cultural, or
  professional judgment; or
- guarantee trust, reciprocity, response, opportunity, repair, or longevity.

## Relationship to Influence

The Relationship Operating Framework and the [Influence Operating Framework
v1.0.0](https://github.com/BradGroux/influence-operating-framework/tree/v1.0.0)
are independent.

Influence governs responsible participation, useful contribution, and how
ideas, people, and communities may move. Relationship governs continuity,
commitments, consent, boundaries, repair, and human stewardship across time.

Contribution can strengthen a relationship, and a relationship can shape how a
person participates. That overlap does not transfer authority between the
frameworks. Relationship does not adopt Influence's concerns, moves, outcome
model, or community-growth method. Influence does not define Relationship's
stewardship practice.

## Commons adoption and independence

This framework adopts [Open Framework Commons
v1.0.0](https://github.com/BradGroux/open-framework-commons/tree/v1.0.0)
exactly. The adopted annotated tag peels to
[`a0f0d384e9010a65d1a21a324b4c912433d5e031`](https://github.com/BradGroux/open-framework-commons/commit/a0f0d384e9010a65d1a21a324b4c912433d5e031).

Commons owns the ecosystem's shared principles and boundaries. Relationship
owns its local purpose, audience, practice, terminology, examples, research,
governance, and releases. A Commons change does not automatically change this
framework. A Relationship change does not amend Commons or another ecosystem
product.

If local guidance must ever deviate from an adopted Commons revision, the
deviation must name the exact shared statement, explain why Relationship needs
different guidance, receive explicit steward approval, and appear in a
Relationship release. The initial release records no deviations.

## Proposals are not doctrine

Ideas about a first adopter, first practical job, relationship lifecycle,
domain model, Mission Control, or community extensions remain proposals. They
may be researched or discussed in issues, but they do not become framework
requirements until accepted through governance and incorporated into canonical
documents.

## Accountability and authority

The founding steward is accountable for framework meaning and releases. People,
teams, contributors, researchers, and agents may apply, challenge, draft, and
review the framework. They do not acquire authority to change it implicitly.

The charter is the highest public authority in this repository. Governance
defines how change is decided. Canonical documents under `framework/` state the
current method. Examples, research, and review records cannot amend any of them.

## Amendment

A charter amendment requires a public proposal, explanation of consequences,
review against the adopted Commons revision and this framework's boundaries,
documented steward decision, disclosure of material dissent, and an identifiable
semantic release. No example, tool, agent action, research note, review report,
or commercial practice may amend the charter silently.
