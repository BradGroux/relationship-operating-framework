# Code of Conduct

## Commitment

Participation in this project should leave people, relationships, and shared
work stronger. Contributors, maintainers, reviewers, and stewards are expected
to treat one another with dignity, preserve consent and privacy, and disagree
without manipulation or retaliation.

## Expected conduct

- Be direct, respectful, and specific about ideas and evidence.
- Distinguish observed facts, interpretation, experience, proposals, and open
  questions.
- Respect communication boundaries, attribution, consent, confidentiality, and
  requests to disengage.
- Give credit, disclose material interests, and correct mistakes promptly.
- Keep real relationship details and sensitive reports out of public threads.
- Make space for different cultures, communication styles, abilities,
  disciplines, and levels of experience.
- Accept waiting, declining, closure, and no response without pressure.

## Unacceptable conduct

- Harassment, threats, intimidation, discrimination, sexualized conduct, or
  personal attacks.
- Doxxing, exposing private messages, or sharing personal or confidential
  information without appropriate permission.
- Fabricated familiarity, deceptive outreach, coercion, retaliation, spam, or
  repeated contact after a boundary.
- Ranking a person's worth by status, access, response, reach, revenue, or
  predicted value.
- Misrepresenting evidence, review, authority, identity, affiliation, consent,
  or another person's views.
- Using project participation to create hidden commercial, social, or personal
  obligation.

## Scope

This code applies in repository spaces and in public or private interactions
when someone is representing the project. It does not grant the project
authority over unrelated personal conduct.

## Reporting and enforcement

Do not place sensitive conduct reports in a public issue or pull request. Use
the private path in the [security and privacy policy](SECURITY.md). The founding
steward or a future designated conduct steward will review reports, limit access,
seek only necessary context, avoid conflicts when practical, and record a
proportionate decision.

Possible responses include clarification, correction, content removal, a
boundary on participation, temporary restriction, or permanent removal from
project spaces. Immediate safety, consent, privacy, and legal needs take
precedence over repository process.
