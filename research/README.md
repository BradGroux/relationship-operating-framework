# Research and evidence

This directory is the public home for source-grounded research that may inform
the Relationship Operating Framework. Research does not become doctrine merely
by being published here.

## Evidence labels

Every material claim or recommendation should use the strongest honest label it
has earned:

### Principle

A normative commitment accepted through framework governance. A principle says
how the framework intends people to practice; it is not an empirical claim that
the practice always works.

### Experience

A contextual observation from practice. Record who is authorized to share it,
the setting, what was observed, the limits of recollection or measurement, and
plausible alternative interpretations. One experience does not establish a
general rule.

### Proposal

An idea offered for research, testing, or governance. It is not current
framework guidance. Proposals should state the question, intended benefit,
risks, alternatives, evidence needed, and what would cause rejection.

### Validated finding

A bounded conclusion supported by an explicit question, appropriate method,
inspectable evidence, relevant review, and stated limitations. Validation is
specific to its context. It does not make a finding universal, permanent, or
professional certification.

## What is not evidence

- A fictional example shows whether guidance can be understood; it does not
  prove effectiveness.
- A documentation review can find ambiguity or misuse risk; it does not prove
  real-world relationship quality.
- Repetition, popularity, model confidence, a search ranking, or inclusion in a
  CRM does not validate a claim.
- A public fact is not automatically appropriate to collect, combine, retain,
  or use in a relationship decision.
- A person's response, silence, role, reach, or commercial value does not prove
  the health or worth of a relationship.

## Research record standard

A public research note should contain:

1. **Status and evidence label** — Principle, Experience, Proposal, or Validated
   finding.
2. **Question** — the relationship-stewardship question being examined.
3. **Source claim** — what each source actually supports.
4. **Example or evidence** — what was observed, measured, or illustrated.
5. **Interpretation** — what the contributor thinks the evidence may mean.
6. **Open question** — what remains unknown or disputed.
7. **Method and scope** — how the work was conducted and where it applies.
8. **Privacy and consent** — why the material may be used and published.
9. **Limitations and dissent** — what the work cannot establish.
10. **Framework consequence** — no change, a proposal, or a governed canonical
    change.

Source claim, evidence, interpretation, and open question must remain visibly
separate. Do not invent quotations, results, participants, metrics, validation,
or professional review.

## Relationship research boundaries

Prefer public, community-owned, generalized, consented, or properly licensed
sources. Use the minimum personal context needed for the research question.
Remove or withhold real names, contact details, private messages, inferred
attributes, confidential material, and sensitive relationship history unless
publication is explicitly authorized and necessary.

Research involving vulnerable people, employment, health, finance, legal
matters, safeguarding, covert observation, or consequential profiling requires
appropriate human and domain oversight outside this framework. If the necessary
authority, consent, or safety cannot be established, do not conduct or publish
the work.

## Initial proposal register

The initial release deliberately leaves these as proposals:

- a first adopter or user profile;
- a first practical job or entry scenario;
- any relationship lifecycle or maturity model;
- any domain model, record shape, status taxonomy, or score;
- any Mission Control capabilities, views, metrics, or design guidance; and
- any extension registry or verification process.

Use real application evidence to decide whether these ideas solve recurring
problems. Do not add them because another framework or product has them.
