# Non-transactional re-engagement

- **Status:** Illustrative
- **Provenance:** Fictional
- **Review limit:** Documentation and framework alignment only; not
  domain-validated or tested in real practice

## Stewardship question

Should a practitioner re-engage a former collaborator after three quiet years
when there is no current project and no right to the other person's attention?

## Fictional context

Morgan and Riley worked together once on a volunteer accessibility guide. At
the end of that work, Riley invited occasional email updates about related
public resources. Morgan promised to share a cleaned-up checklist but never
closed the loop. They have not spoken since, and Morgan now has a new guide that
might be relevant.

An assisting tool can find the old promise in Morgan's authorized project
notes. It cannot establish whether the old invitation still reflects Riley's
current preference.

## Applying the framework

### What relationship actually exists?

There was one real collaboration and an invitation for occasional related
updates. Morgan does not describe Riley as a close friend, active partner, warm
lead, or likely buyer.

### What is already owed?

The unfinished checklist comes before any new request. Morgan confirms the
promise against the original note and checks whether the promised material is
still useful and safe to share.

### What is welcome?

The prior channel and subject were explicit, but the permission is old. Morgan
limits the decision to one proportionate message and includes no automated
follow-up. Any withdrawal, delivery failure, or do-not-contact signal ends the
attempt.

### What can be contributed without debt?

Morgan can acknowledge the missed follow-through, share the relevant public
resource, and make clear that no reply is needed. The contribution does not
purchase a meeting, introduction, endorsement, or commercial conversation.

### What should a person decide?

Morgan, not the assisting tool, reviews the exact recipient, context, channel,
wording, attachment, and timing. A bounded decision could be:

> Send one plain message that acknowledges the late follow-through, links the
> resource, and leaves Riley free not to respond. Make no new ask and schedule
> no retry.

Waiting or not contacting would also be valid if Morgan cannot confirm the
channel, the promise, or the relevance.

## Where the example stops

The example does not claim that Morgan sends the message, that Riley responds,
or that the relationship improves. It demonstrates a responsible decision
boundary, not an outcome.
