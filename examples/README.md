# Examples

These fictional examples show how the Relationship Operating Framework can
support ordinary stewardship decisions without software, scoring, fabricated
results, or hidden context.

Examples are explanatory. They cannot amend the [charter](../framework/charter.md),
[principles and boundaries](../framework/principles-and-boundaries.md), or
[practice guide](../framework/practice-guide.md).

## How to read them

Each example states:

- its fictional provenance and review limits;
- the stewardship question;
- only the context needed for the example;
- how the framework changes the decision; and
- where the example stops without inventing an outcome.

The names, organizations, facts, decisions, and messages are fictional. The
examples have been reviewed only for documentation quality and framework
alignment. They are not evidence that the method works, domain validation,
professional advice, or authorization to copy an action into a real
relationship.

## Collection

1. [Non-transactional re-engagement](01-non-transactional-reengagement.md) — a
   person reconnects after a long quiet period without hiding an ask or
   inventing closeness.
2. [Continuity during a team handoff](02-continuity-during-a-team-handoff.md) — a
   team transfers stewardship without treating a personal relationship as
   organizational property.
3. [Assisted commitment follow-through](03-assisted-commitment-follow-through.md)
   — an agent surfaces possible commitments while a human preserves consent,
   verifies context, and controls every external action.

## Publication boundary

Do not contribute real relationship notes, contact details, private messages,
inferred personal attributes, or confidential organizational context. A real
example requires explicit steward approval, a reason fiction cannot serve the
same reader job, permission and sourcing appropriate to the material, and
publication-safety review.
