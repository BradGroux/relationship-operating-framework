## Changes and compatibility

Help, clarification, accommodation and support do not have to be earned through
prior contribution. The framework now explicitly considers unequal power,
finite capacity, conflicting obligations and responsible stopping. Sensitive
context stays minimal; disputed interpretations do not become facts, and
retention does not authorize reuse, surveillance or contact.

Silence does not authorize manual chasing, channel switching or approaches
through someone else. An old promise or wish to apologize does not override
withdrawn permission. The handoff example distinguishes refusal of an
introduction from withdrawal of permission for a promised delivery.

These are substantive changes, potentially incompatible with earlier reader
interpretations. Adopters should reconsider earned-help rules, follow-up
practices, no-contact repair, capacity assumptions and retained notes. They
should preserve lawful and authorized obligations through their designated
processes without treating this framework as legal or safeguarding advice.
Human ownership and the prohibition on autonomous outreach remain unchanged.

## Verification and limits

The [audit and disposition](../reviews/edition-2026-09-05-audit.md) records
findings, scenario decisions, review scope and verification. Repository checks
cover public links, calendar metadata and publication hygiene; the release
verifier checks exact annotated objects, commits, author, final state and the
committed release body. [Release operations](releasing.md) describe the
remaining merge and publication gates and historical verification limits.

Examples and adverse cases are fictional. No longitudinal practitioner evidence,
specialist validation, effectiveness claim or external adoption is established.
No downloadable assets are required. The release tag identifies the exact
merged commit; publication is complete only after remote readback.
