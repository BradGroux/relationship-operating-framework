## Executive summary

The framework was usable in ordinary prose without private planning context,
specialized software, a score, or a prescribed lifecycle. Four fictional
exercises supported bounded decisions while keeping external action under human
ownership and preserving waiting, closure, no action, and do not contact as
legitimate outcomes.

No exercise required a practitioner to invent a relationship, infer consent,
retain unnecessary context, or claim that an interaction succeeded.

## Application evidence

### Long-quiet re-engagement

A practitioner can establish the real shared history, identify an overdue
promise, examine stale permission, and choose one transparent message or wait
or not contact. The example forbids retries and makes no claim of response or
improvement (`framework/practice-guide.md:75-93`;
`examples/01-non-transactional-reengagement.md:25-68`).

### Team handoff with private context

The guidance separates organizational promises and authorized work context
from a departing steward's private relationship. It requires consent for an
introduction and permits refusal or closure without transferring intimacy
(`framework/practice-guide.md:115-129`;
`examples/02-continuity-during-a-team-handoff.md:26-63`).

### Overdue commitment and repair

A practitioner can confirm the promise, pause new asks, name a human owner,
disclose changed capacity, renegotiate or close, and offer proportionate repair
without demanding engagement or forgiveness
(`framework/principles-and-boundaries.md:28-36`;
`framework/practice-guide.md:61-73`;
`framework/practice-guide.md:95-113`).

### Bounded assistance and do not contact

An agent may surface authorized context but cannot infer permission or initiate
action. The human rejects batch outreach, preserves the controlling
do-not-contact boundary, and applies exact-action review only to the confirmed
commitment (`framework/practice-guide.md:131-148`;
`examples/03-assisted-commitment-follow-through.md:27-60`).
